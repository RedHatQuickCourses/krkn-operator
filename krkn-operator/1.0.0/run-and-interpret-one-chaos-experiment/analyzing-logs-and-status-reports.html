Analyzing logs and status reports

Analyzing Logs and Status Reports

After executing a Krkn chaos experiment, the validation phase begins. Analyzing the results is critical to understanding how your system behaved during the disruption, whether your observability tools fired the correct alerts, and if the recovery process occurred within your defined Service Level Objectives (SLOs).

Understanding Krkn Execution Reports

When a Krkn experiment concludes, the Krkn Operator generates a comprehensive report that summarizes the execution lifecycle. This report is your primary artifact for post-mortem analysis.

Component Description

Status

Indicates the final outcome: Completed, Failed, or Aborted. A Failed status often points to a configuration error or a lost connection to the target cluster.

Duration

The total time elapsed during the disruption, including the stabilization period.

Log Aggregation

Provides access to the stdout of the scenario execution, detailing the specific disruption steps (e.g., specific pod kills or network latency injection).

Metrics Summary

Displays the captured state of your key indicators if integrated with your observability stack.

Interpreting Chaos Logs

Logs generated by Krkn provide granular visibility into the disruption sequence. When reviewing logs:

  1. Check Initialization: Ensure the scenario correctly identified the target pods or nodes.

  2. Verify Disruption Timing: Look for timestamps of when the chaos injection started and stopped. This is essential for correlating disruptions with application errors in your logs (e.g., ELK stack, Grafana Loki).

  3. Identify Failures during Injection: If the scenario log shows retries or timeouts while attempting to impact a target, it may indicate that your blast radius is restricted by security policies or that the target resource was already unavailable.

Evaluating Status Reports and Observability

A chaos experiment is only as valuable as the data captured during its execution. To perform an effective analysis:

  • Correlate with Alerts: Verify that your alerting system triggered for the correct services during the experiment. If you injected high latency but no latency-related alerts fired, your observability rules require tuning.

  • Compare Against Steady State: Use the metrics gathered during the experiment to compare against the "steady state" defined before the test.

  • Validate Recovery: Check if your system returned to normal operational parameters automatically without manual intervention.

If you have configured a Resiliency Score for your workflow, look at the scoring breakdown in the Jobs page. The score provides a summarized "health" trend based on the PromQL queries defined in your workflow, allowing you to quickly spot deviations from your performance thresholds.

Hands-on Activity: Analyzing Experiment Results

In this activity, you will review the output of a recently executed pod-disruption experiment.

  1. Locate the Job: Navigate to the Jobs section in the Krkn dashboard.

  2. Select the Execution: Click on the most recent completed experiment.

  3. Review Logs:

    • Open the log view.

    • Search for the specific string identifying the affected pod (e.g., Killing pod: <pod-name>).

    • Verify the time taken for the Ready state of the pod to return to True post-disruption.

  4. Analyze Metrics:

    • Open the Metrics tab associated with the job.

    • Compare the latency graph against the start/stop time found in your logs.

    • Determine if there was a spike that exceeded your pre-defined threshold.

  5. Documentation: Record whether the system recovery was successful and if the alert duration matched the disruption duration.

If your analysis shows that recovery took longer than expected, document the bottleneck (e.g., slow container startup time or inefficient health checks) and propose a remediation plan for the next iteration.
