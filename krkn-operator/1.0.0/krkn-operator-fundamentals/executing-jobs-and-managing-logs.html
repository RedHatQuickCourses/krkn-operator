Executing jobs and managing logs

Executing jobs and managing logs

In the Krkn ecosystem, the lifecycle of a chaos experiment centers on the Jobs interface. This is where your hypotheses meet reality, allowing you to monitor real-time disruptions, inspect granular execution data, and troubleshoot unexpected outcomes.

Executing Chaos Experiments

When you launch a scenario, you transition from configuration to execution. The Krkn platform automates the orchestration of these experiments, managing the lifecycle of the disruption pods, target resource isolation, and data collection.

Launching a Job

To initiate an experiment: 1. Navigate to your desired scenario within the registry. 2. Configure the experiment parameters (blast radius, duration, and target namespace/labels). 3. Click Run. 4. You will be automatically redirected to the Jobs dashboard.

Monitoring Real-Time Status

The Jobs dashboard provides a high-level view of all scenario executions within your group. Key metrics displayed include: * Status: (Pending, Running, Succeeded, Failed, or Interrupted). * Execution Timestamp: Start and end times for duration tracking. * Cluster Targets: The specific infrastructure or application targets affected by the run.

Managing and Inspecting Logs

Once a job is running or completed, managing the output is critical for validating whether your service maintained its steady state.

Accessing Console Logs

To inspect the internal behavior of the Krkn operator during the execution: 1. Locate the specific job in the Jobs list. 2. Select the Logs tab associated with that job. 3. Review the output for: * Disruption initiation: Confirming that the targeted resources were successfully impacted. * Recovery sequence: Observing the automated cleanup or workload recovery steps. * Errors/Warnings: Identifying issues with permissions, connectivity, or invalid resource selectors.

Permissions and Visibility

The Krkn platform enforces a granular security model regarding job management: * View Access: Users with "View" permissions can inspect job outcomes, graph run nodes, and Resiliency Scores, but are restricted from executing or deleting experiments. * Group Isolation: By default, the dashboard displays only jobs associated with your specific group. * Cluster-Wide Transparency: A cluster-wide indicator shows the names of scenarios running across other groups, providing visibility without compromising the security of unauthorized jobs. * Deletion: Removing historical job data requires explicit "Delete" permissions to ensure audit trails remain intact.

Hands-on Activity: Observing a Job Lifecycle

In this activity, you will execute a pod-disruption experiment and verify the logs to confirm the system’s behavior.

  1. Launch the Experiment:

    • Select a standard pod-disruption scenario from your registry.

    • Set a duration of 120 seconds.

    • Click Run.

  2. Monitor Execution:

    • Observe the status change from Pending to Running on the Jobs page.

    • Refresh the page to see the real-time status updates as the chaos progresses.

  3. Inspect Logs:

    • Once the status reaches Succeeded, click on the job ID.

    • Navigate to the Logs tab.

    • Search for the log entry Disruption successfully initiated and verify the recovery timestamp to see if the workload scaled back to its original steady state.

  4. Analyze Results:

    • Review the Resiliency Score generated post-execution. If the score is lower than expected, cross-reference the console logs with your application metrics to identify the specific moment of SLO degradation.
