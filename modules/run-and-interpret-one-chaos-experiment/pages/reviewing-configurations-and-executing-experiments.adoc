#  Reviewing configurations and executing experiments

= Reviewing configurations and executing experiments

Before injecting disruptions into a production or staging environment, it is critical to perform a final validation of your experiment parameters. The Krkn Operator provides a structured preview mechanism to ensure that your "blast radius" is contained and your expected steady-state metrics are clearly defined.

== The importance of the pre-execution review

Executing a chaos experiment without a rigorous configuration review can lead to unintended consequences, such as exceeding the defined blast radius or triggering false-positive alerts. The review phase serves as the "stop condition" gatekeeper, allowing you to verify:

* **Target Accuracy:** Confirm the target cluster, namespace, and specific resource labels (e.g., `app=frontend`) match the intended scope.
* **Disruption Intensity:** Validate that parameters such as `duration`, `grace_period`, and `concurrency` align with your hypothesis.
* **Safety Mechanisms:** Ensure that your monitoring tools (Prometheus/Grafana) are configured to observe the target and that automated rollback or "stop" conditions are primed.

== Steps to review and execute

When using the Krkn Operator interface, follow this systematic approach to transition from configuration to execution.

=== 1. The Configuration Summary (Preview)
Before the `Run` command is finalized, the Krkn Operator generates a **Configuration Summary**. This interface aggregates all inputs provided in the scenario registry:

* **Scope Check:** Identify which clusters and nodes will be impacted.
* **Parameter Validation:** Review key-value pairs for the disruption (e.g., `pod_failure_percentage: 20`).
* **Dependency Check:** Verify that the required service account permissions and cluster discovery mappings are correctly resolved for the target cluster.

=== 2. Executing the Experiment
Once the summary is validated, the execution process triggers the underlying Kubernetes Jobs.

* **Initialization:** Krkn Operator initializes the chaos engine pod within the target namespace.
* **Observability Sync:** The system begins streaming real-time status updates back to the Krkn dashboard.
* **Steady-State Monitoring:** The experiment begins by capturing the current state (Latency, Error Rate, Throughput) to establish a baseline before the disruption starts.

=== 3. Monitoring Execution
During execution, utilize the Krkn terminal and logs interface:
* **Real-time Logs:** Access the pod logs to verify that the scenario is interacting with the target resources as expected.
* **Status Reports:** Track the progress percentage and current stage of the chaos workflow.

[IMPORTANT]
====
If at any point the system metrics deviate beyond your defined SLO thresholds, trigger the manual abort mechanism via the Krkn dashboard to immediately revert the environment to a stable state.
====

== Hands-on Activity: Executing a Pod-Disruption Experiment

In this activity, you will verify the configuration of a pod-disruption scenario and execute it against a test namespace.

**Prerequisites:**
* Access to a Krkn-enabled cluster.
* A target application deployed in a namespace (e.g., `demo-app`).

. **Step 1: Configuration Preview**
   - Navigate to the Krkn Operator **Run Scenarios** tab.
   - Select the `pod-disruption` scenario.
   - Configure the blast radius to target `namespace: demo-app`.
   - Click **Preview** to load the summary modal.

. **Step 2: Validation**
   - Inspect the summary modal. Ensure the `target_label` matches your test application.
   - Confirm that the `duration` is set to `5m` (5 minutes).

. **Step 3: Execution**
   - Click **Execute**.
   - Navigate to the **Jobs** section to observe the pod spin-up.

. **Step 4: Observation**
   - Open your Prometheus dashboard or the Krkn **Resilience Score** view.
   - Observe how the `error_rate` metrics shift as the pods are terminated and restarted by the Kubernetes controller.

. **Step 5: Post-Mortem**
   - Once the experiment completes, review the generated status report.
   - Verify that the workload successfully recovered within the expected time frame.
